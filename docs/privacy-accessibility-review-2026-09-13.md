# Smurfius.com privacy and accessibility review

Date: September 13, 2026. Operator supplied by owner: **Smurfius.com**, Alberta, Canada. Public contact: **btherio99@proton.me**.

## Result and deployment scope

The current GhotiCMS installation now publishes a privacy policy, accessibility notice, permission-aware HTML sitemap, and optional analytics choices through every bundled theme's footer. The installation was confirmed at **https://ghoti.smurfius.com/**. The separate **https://smurfius.com/** installation serves an older copy from a different directory; it has not been overwritten by this work.

This is a technical improvement and an applicability review, **not a certification of legal compliance or complete WCAG conformance**. A policy cannot establish that business procedures, contractual safeguards, advertising integrations, payment processing, every stored page, and historical records meet all applicable requirements. Remaining items below are material.

## Applicable frameworks

|---|---|
| Alberta PIPA | A primary framework to assess for an Alberta private-sector operator handling personal information. Confirm the legal entity and the scope of the organization's activities. [OIPC overview](https://oipc.ab.ca/legislation/pipa/) |
| PIPEDA | Relevant to commercial personal information crossing provincial or national borders, and applicable federally regulated activities. Alberta's substantially similar law does not eliminate every PIPEDA obligation. [OPC business guide](https://www.priv.gc.ca/media/2038/guide_org_e.pdf) |
| GDPR | Mere availability to EU visitors does not alone establish applicability. Offering goods/services to people in the EU or monitoring their behaviour can bring a non-EU operator into scope. ## Implemented controls

- **Notice and contact:** policy explains accounts, contributions, operational information, optional analytics, purposes, consent, service providers, payments/ads, international processing, retention limitations, rights and complaints. Contact details are editable in Site Settings and have been saved for this installation. Registration links to the notice at collection.
- **Choice before optional analytics:** no new usage events before a positive server-side session choice. Equal-action Enable/Decline controls live in the footer; declining does not block the site. Withdrawal clears the analytics identifier and stops future tracking. GPC and DNT prevent collection, including when a positive choice is submitted. The preference expires with the session; it is not a durable cross-device preference.
- **Data minimization:** new analytics rows omit account ID, IP address, raw user-agent and request URI. Browser/device categories are derived before discarding the raw string; referrers retain only the hostname. A random analytics identifier replaces the authentication session ID. Analytics remains pseudonymous usage data, not a claim of irreversible anonymization. Historical rows are unchanged.
- **Third-party theme requests:** Syne, IBM Plex Mono and Inter fonts, GSAP, ScrollTrigger and Lenis are served locally. Duplicate CDN jQuery loads are removed. Font licences and dependency provenance are included. Editor-supplied remote content is a separate concern.
- **Permission-aware sitemap:** server queries only public pages for guests and adds private pages for authenticated users. Titles are escaped. Private titles are not hidden in delivered HTML; they are excluded before delivery. The CMS currently supports public and signed-in page groups, not arbitrary per-user ACLs. Unknown groups fail closed. Page-by-ID and title endpoints use the same visibility rule.
- **Cache protection:** normal page responses are private/no-store; the personalized sitemap also sends noindex/nofollow. This is a human-facing sitemap, not an XML feed of private content.
- **Accessibility improvements:** server-rendered notices and sitemap; labelled footer navigation; visible keyboard focus; skip links; live feedback; semantic dialog, background inertness, focus containment, Escape dismissal and focus restoration; reduced-motion handling; improved navigation contrast and corrected legacy nested-list markup.

## Verification

- `php tests/privacy.php` passes without a database: guest/member authorization, no unknown-group leakage, title escaping, authorized sitemap queries, strict consent input, default-off tracking, withdrawal, GPC/DNT, and the actual analytics insert payload.
- PHP lint and JavaScript syntax checks passed for changed application code.
- Headless Chromium tested actual PHP pages against the local installation using the MySQL driver, rather than only static mockups. Five themes were exercised at mobile width for policy rendering, footer links, overflow and JavaScript errors. Prosimii's dialog focus/return and Decline control were exercised; sitemap rendering was checked.
- axe-core checked the privacy article under WCAG A/AA tags in all five themes with no reported violations. A broader scan found existing navigation contrast and malformed list issues; these were repaired and rescanned. See `accessibility-scan-2026-09-13.json` for final desktop results.
- These checks do not establish conformance for every editor-authored page, authenticated admin workflow, image description, document, video, browser, assistive technology, zoom mode or error state. Manual screen-reader testing and representative task testing remain necessary.
- Public GET verification confirmed the privacy policy with supplied contact details on the Ghoti subdomain. No authenticated production credentials were used for the tests.

## Required follow-through

1. **Name and review payment and advertising providers.** The owner confirmed ads and outsourced payments; provider names and deployment details remain pending. Identify data fields, cookies, countries, contracts, privacy notices and any ad tags. The inspected bundled banners use local images with outbound links. That inventory does not prove that editor content or the separate main site has no external tracking. Optional analytics consent does not gate arbitrary ad scripts.
2. **Set and implement a retention schedule.** Existing analytics may contain raw IP addresses, user-agent strings, account IDs and authentication session IDs. No historical data was deleted in this change. Accounts, comments and analytics have no automatic expiry; logs rotate by size; host/mail/backup schedules are unverified. Decide which historical data must be erased or anonymized and configure ongoing expiry. New data minimization does not retrospectively clean old records. The public policy explicitly describes the current limitation rather than inventing a 30/90-day promise. [OPC retention guidance](https://www.priv.gc.ca/media/2038/guide_org_e.pdf)
3. **Operate the privacy-request process.** Monitor the published mailbox, designate a responsible person, verify identity proportionately, search applicable systems, handle access/correction/deletion requests, document exceptions and meet applicable deadlines. A contact link is not evidence that this process exists. [OPC transparency guidance](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/business-privacy/collecting-personal-information/pp_info_201310/?wbdisable=true)
4. **Assess foreign service providers.** Confirm hosting, backups, email and payment processing locations. The supplied contact is a Proton mailbox; its role, processing locations and contractual terms have not been audited. Alberta has specific notification requirements when using service providers outside Canada. If GDPR applies, evaluate transfer mechanisms and any representative/DPO requirements rather than assuming Canadian hosting is sufficient. [Alberta responsibilities](https://www.alberta.ca/organization-responsibilities-for-protecting-personal-information)
5. **Maintain an incident plan.** Document detection, containment, risk assessment, evidence preservation and required notification. Alberta PIPA requires reporting qualifying breaches to the Commissioner; other applicable laws can add duties. [OIPC breach guidance](https://oipc.ab.ca/breach-notification/)
6. **Finish accessibility review of actual content and tasks.** Check screen-reader reading order, keyboard-only account recovery, errors, zoom/reflow, forms, meaningful alt text, captions and documents. Train editors and provide an accessible support process. The published accessibility notice explicitly avoids an unverified conformance claim.
7. **Review security deployment separately.** Earlier security remediation exists on a separate review branch. This work does not establish that every finding from that broader review has been merged into every deployment. Include patching, access control, backups and restore tests in operations.
8. **Review the actual legal entity and final notice.** The owner supplied “Smurfius.com” as the legal name. Confirm whether it identifies the legal person responsible, and have the final operational facts and jurisdictional analysis reviewed before representing the site as fully compliant.

No marketing-consent promises, payment-provider names, fixed retention periods, breach-response time commitments, or claims of universal compliance were invented to fill missing facts.
